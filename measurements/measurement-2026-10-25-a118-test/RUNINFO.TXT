 Own host tests (method/tester/aes_tester_extra-appended-tests.rs.txt, appended to a copy of the developer's test file in a scratch tree):
  t_wind_set_parked_then_window_deleted_does_not_leak_lock, t_wind_open_parked_then_..._opened_by_other_..., t_wind_close_parked_then_..._closed_by_other_...
  all three RED on 810fb347: B parks behind A's hold, A deletes/opens/closes the window and ends its hold, the lock is handed to B,
  B's re-executed call refuses (ret 0) and takes no lock -> "B re-exec ret=Some(0) lock=(1, Some(1))" (expected (0, None)).
  Cause: window::opcode_takes_lock (mod.rs gate) is state-dependent (win(h) exists / open); update_block's hand-over (UPDATE_GRANTED) is only
  consumed by tak_flag in update_beg, which the refused body never calls. EmuTOS has no such case: the validity checks are before the
  wm_update and the blocked process continues as the holder. Effect: the lock stays held by B until B's next taker call (tak_flag
  consumes the grant) -- other processes' wind_update/fm_own/... park meanwhile. Needs another process to change the window between park
  and hand-over; not seen in any real app. Not present on base (base never parked these opcodes).

   The takers, their positions (after the refusals, bracket around the body, ap_exit held across mn_cleanup + wait_for_accs, END after the
   drain) match; the two named deviations (fsel waits before drawing; wm_new_for_slot counts on top) are as stated and harmless:
   the fsel modal loop and the alert loop give the CPU to no one, so no lock is held across a park.
   Deadlock review: no lock is held across a park except ap_exit's (as in EmuTOS; wait_for_accs is bounded by WAIT_TIMEOUT); an accessory
   answering AC_CLOSE with BEG_UPDATE parks until ap_exit's END and then gets the hand-over (shown in QEMU, section 4d). Re-entrancy of a
   DA inside fsel cannot occur (modal loop never yields). The one design flaw is the double evaluation of section 1.
